xPresence (expected presence) is the chance a champion gets picked or banned in a fresh Game 1 draft. Regular presence just counts how often a champion was picked or banned. xPresence estimates that rate from every real pick and ban, giving credit each time a team took the champion while other options were still available. It adjusts for fearless, so a champion used earlier in a series isn't penalized for being unavailable later, and recent games count more: a game loses half its weight every 5 days. Patch-adjusted goes one step further and nudges champions changed in the patches since this data (16.18 and 16.19) up for each buff and down for each nerf.

          How xPresence works

          • Every pick or ban is a choice among the champions still legal at that moment: not banned, not already picked in the game, and not used earlier in the series.
          • A Plackett–Luce model fits one strength per champion that best explains all 15,000+ choices. Taking a champion while strong options remain counts for more than taking it from a thin pool, so draft order and fearless are built in without hand-set weights.
          • xPresence turns strength into a plain number: the share of 3,000 simulated fresh Game 1 drafts in which the champion is picked or banned.
          • Each game is weighted by recency: it loses half its weight every 5 days before the latest game. Until Sep 26 this was 10 days, with deciding games counted 1.5×; the rolling tests showed 5 days does better and deciding games make no difference.
          • Patch-adjusted multiplies a champion's strength by 1.25 for each buff and divides it by 1.25 for each nerf in patches released after the data (currently 16.18 and 16.19; the Worlds patch, 16.20, is due Oct 7). Champions no team drafted this summer have no strength to adjust, so a buff can't lift them onto the list. Unchanged champions shift slightly too, because a nerf to one champion frees up draft slots for everyone else.
          • The 80% range comes from refitting the model on 40 resamples of whole series.

          Settings

          This changes xPresence on every tab. The default of 5 days comes from the rolling tests on the Evidence tab.

          Deciding-game weighting was dropped on Sep 26: it had no measurable effect in 31 tests. Patch-notes adjustment: ×1.25 per buff, ÷1.25 per nerf.

              About the data

              • 775 games in 284 series from each league's post-MSI split: LCK Rounds 3–4, Play-In and Playoffs; LPL Split 3, Grand Finals and Regional Finals; LEC Summer and Summer Playoffs; LCS Summer and Playoffs; CBLOL Split 2 and Playoffs; LCP Split 3. LCP Split 3 playoffs weren't available yet. Play-ins, playoffs and regional finals count as postseason.
              • Source: Oracle's Elixir 2026 match data (Sep 26 file) for 762 games, with full pick order, first pick, side and bans. The other 13 come from gol.gg because Oracle's Elixir doesn't have them yet: the 12 LPL Regional Finals games and one LPL game (Team WE vs ThunderTalk, Aug 7, Game 3). One of those 13 has no pick order and gets the average weight.
              • Fearless holds in every league: no champion was picked twice in any series (see table).
              • Cross-checking gol.gg against Oracle's Elixir fixed: 56 games where gol.gg listed picks by role instead of draft order (most in LPL), 30 LPL games missing the first-pick marker, 2 picks with a blank champion, and the LCK Gen.G vs Dplus KIA series on Aug 1, where gol.gg's Game 1 was a copy of Game 2 with sides swapped (Oracle's Elixir has only Game 2, which is what's used). In one LPL game (NIP vs IG, Aug 22, Game 2) Oracle's Elixir listed picks as bans, so gol.gg's bans are kept.
              • gol.gg rank comes from rebuilding PrioScore with gol.gg's published rule (ban 1, pick rounds 1 / ½ / ⅓). Pick and ban counts match gol.gg; the rebuilt score lands within about 4 points of theirs.
